The Great Green Wall: Is Africa Really Becoming Greener?

Project Overview

The Great Green Wall initiative is one of Africa's largest environmental restoration efforts, aimed at combating land degradation, desertification, and climate vulnerability across the Sahel region.

This project explores the question: "Is Africa's Great Green Wall really becoming greener?"

Using Earth Observation concepts and geospatial analysis, the project communicates how satellite-based monitoring can support the assessment of vegetation recovery, land restoration progress, and ecosystem resilience.

Project Objectives

  • Explore the role of GIS and Remote Sensing in monitoring large-scale restoration initiatives.
  • Demonstrate how satellite observations can be used to assess vegetation changes over time.
  • Highlight the importance of Earth Observation data in tracking climate adaptation and land restoration efforts.
  • Communicate environmental insights through geospatial visualization.

Methodology

1. Earth Observation Data

Satellite-based environmental information was explored to understand vegetation dynamics and landscape changes.

Data Sources:

  • Sentinel-2 Multispectral Imagery
  • Landsat Satellite Imagery
  • Vegetation indices (NDVI)
  • Land cover datasets

2. Vegetation Monitoring

Vegetation conditions were assessed using spectral indicators such as:

NDVI (Normalized Difference Vegetation Index)

NDVI was used to identify vegetation presence, density, and changes across landscapes.

Formula:

NDVI = (NIR - Red) / (NIR + Red)

Where:

  • NIR represents near-infrared reflectance
  • Red represents visible red reflectance

3. Spatial Interpretation

Satellite observations were interpreted to understand:

  • Vegetation recovery patterns
  • Areas experiencing degradation
  • The relationship between restoration efforts and landscape conditions

Geospatial Tools Used

Remote Sensing Platforms

  • Google Earth Engine
  • Sentinel-2 Imagery
  • Landsat Archive

GIS & Visualization

  • ArcGIS Pro
  • QGIS

Key Insights

The project demonstrates that:

  • Satellite data provides an effective approach for monitoring environmental restoration.
  • Vegetation trends can reveal areas of ecological improvement or continued land degradation.
  • GIS enables evidence-based evaluation of climate adaptation and restoration programs.
